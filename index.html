When stopped, a track's REC button counts in and records from the playback position. While playing, it waits for the top of the loop (or the next bar) and records exactly one pass. Each track holds a clip. Recording on an empty track makes a clip of the bars you recorded, so with a 2-bar section looping you get a 2-bar clip that repeats across the whole form. Drag the clip to move it, or drag its edges to crop it. The dimmed repeats before and after it can each be switched off by hovering over them. Overdubbing inside the clip layers onto it, so the new part repeats too. Overdubbing beyond it turns the repeats into real audio first, so nothing changes sound. Click any track's waveform or the progress bar to jump there. Tracks, chords and settings are kept in this browser between visits.

Projects

Each project keeps its own tracks, chords, tempo and mixer settings. The open project saves itself automatically as you work.

⏱ Calibrate recording timing

You'll hear 4 count-in clicks, then 16 clicks at 100 BPM. Clap, tap the mic, or pick a muted string exactly on each of the 16 clicks, the same way you'd play along while recording.

On speakers instead of headphones? Stay quiet and the mic will pick up the clicks themselves, which measures the hardware delay.
